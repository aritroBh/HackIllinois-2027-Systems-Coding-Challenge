# Code of conduct

This project is built by and for people running hackathons, which means the same standard
applies here as at the event: be someone others are glad to have on the shift.

## What we expect

Be direct about code and kind about people. Criticise the design, the invariant or the
patch, not the person who wrote it. Assume the other person had a reason, and ask what it
was before deciding they did not.

Reviews are where most of the work happens, so keep them useful. Say what is wrong and what
would be right. "This is wrong" without the second half is not a review.

Some people here are students shipping their first real system. Extend the patience you
wanted when you were.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form. Personal attacks, demeaning comments, or comments about someone's
identity rather than their work. Sexual attention or imagery. Publishing anyone's private
information. Sustained disruption of discussion. Encouraging any of the above.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else the project's
work happens, including in private messages that start from it.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainers through GitHub's private vulnerability
reporting on this repository, which is the private channel available to us, or to the
organisers of the event the fork belongs to. Reports are handled discreetly.

Maintainers will respond. That can mean asking someone to change their behaviour, editing
or removing a comment, or removing someone's access to the project. Maintainers who do not
follow this document are held to it the same way.
